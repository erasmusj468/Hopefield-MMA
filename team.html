COACH PROFILE • HEAD COACH

BUILDING BETTER FIGHTERS.

Becoming a good fighter is about more than being tough. The aim is to develop well-rounded, intelligent and adaptable MMA fighters who understand what they are doing, why they are doing it and how to make good decisions under pressure.

01
ABOUT THE COACH

Hands-On Coaching

Gerhardt takes a hands-on approach: demonstrating techniques, working directly with fighters and paying attention to the small details that can make a big difference inside the cage.

02
EVERY FIGHTER IS DIFFERENT

Individual Development

Every fighter has different strengths, weaknesses, physical abilities and personalities. Training is adapted to the individual, developing natural strengths while systematically improving weaknesses that could become a problem in competition.

03
TRAINING WITH A PURPOSE

Realistic MMA Situations

Training uses technical drilling, wrestling, ground work, cage work, conditioning and situational sparring to prepare fighters for situations they may actually face in competition. Sparring is also used to identify problems so specific weaknesses can be taken back into training and improved.

Striking→Close Distance→Takedown→Control→Escape / Finish→Get Back Up
04
TECHNIQUE • TIMING • ADAPTABILITY

Fight IQ Matters

A strong fighter must be able to think. Timing, distance, positioning, technique and the ability to adapt when the original game plan does not work are all important parts of MMA. The goal is to understand the sport, not simply memorise techniques.

05
CONFIDENCE THROUGH PREPARATION

Prepare Body, Skills & Mind

Real confidence comes from preparation. Fighters repeatedly train difficult situations, understand their techniques and learn how to respond when things do not go according to plan.

06
BUILDING FIGHTERS FOR THE LONG TERM

Keep Learning. Keep Improving.

The goal is not simply to produce fighters who can win one fight. Hopefield MMA aims to develop athletes who continue learning, improving and growing every time they step onto the mat — working hard, learning from mistakes, respecting training partners and continuing to move forward.

COACHING PHILOSOPHY
“Don’t just train harder. Train smarter.”
